How many steps a day do you really need?

November 23, 2026

Ten thousand steps a day is the number everyone knows. It makes a good round target, but it isn't where the research draws the line. The studies point to something more useful: the benefit of walking keeps growing as you add steps, then levels off, and where it levels off depends on how old you are.

The benefit levels off, and earlier than 10,000 for most adults

The biggest study so far pooled 15 studies of about 47,000 adults. The risk of dying early fell as daily steps rose, then levelled off at about 6,000 to 8,000 steps for adults 60 and over and 8,000 to 10,000 for younger adults (Paluch et al., The Lancet Public Health, 2022).

So if you're 65 and walking 7,000 steps a day, you're already getting most of what walking has to offer. If you're 30, it's worth aiming a little higher.

Every thousand steps counts, from about 4,000

You don't have to reach a big number before walking starts doing you good. A 2023 review of 17 studies and about 227,000 people found benefits starting at around 4,000 steps a day, with each extra 1,000 steps linked to a 15% lower risk of death (Banach et al., European Journal of Preventive Cardiology, 2023).

Among about 4,800 US adults followed for a decade, 8,000 steps a day was linked to half the risk of death of 4,000 (Saint-Maurice et al., JAMA, 2020).

Under 5,000 is a sedentary day

Fewer than 5,000 steps a day is the widely used marker of a sedentary lifestyle (Tudor-Locke et al., Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism, 2013). If that's where your days usually land, getting above it is the first goal worth having.

Teens need more

For teenagers, roughly 10,000 to 12,000 steps a day lines up with the recommended hour of moderate-to-vigorous activity (Tudor-Locke et al., International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity, 2011).

So what number should you pick?

  • Under 18: about 10,000 to 12,000.
  • 18 to 59: about 8,000 to 10,000.
  • 60 and over: about 6,000 to 8,000.
  • Walking under 5,000 now? Start by getting past it. Every 1,000 you add counts.

A goal you hit most days does more than a bigger one you keep missing. Pick the number for your age, and raise it once it feels easy.
