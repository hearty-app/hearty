How much sleep do you actually need?

November 23, 2026

Most people know they should sleep more. Fewer know how much is enough, or that the answer changes as you get older. Here's what the expert panels and the biggest studies say.

The recommended hours, by age

The National Sleep Foundation's expert panel recommends (Hirshkowitz et al., Sleep Health, 2015):

  • Teens: 8 to 10 hours.
  • Adults 18 to 64: 7 to 9 hours.
  • Adults 65 and over: 7 to 8 hours.

A joint panel from the American Academy of Sleep Medicine and the Sleep Research Society reached the same line for adults: 7 or more hours a night, regularly (Watson et al., Sleep, 2015).

What short sleep is linked to

That same panel concluded that regularly sleeping less than 7 hours is linked to weight gain, diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease and stroke.

The heart link shows up clearly in the numbers. Across 15 studies of nearly 475,000 people, short sleepers had a 48% higher risk of developing or dying from heart disease (Cappuccio et al., European Heart Journal, 2011).

More isn't always better

The same review found that very long sleep was linked to higher risk too. That doesn't mean a long lie-in on Sunday is bad for you. It means the target is a range, not "as much as possible". Regularly sleeping well past the top of your range can be worth mentioning to a doctor.

Making it a habit

  • Pick a bedtime, not just a number. Count back from when you have to get up: for 8 hours before a 7am alarm, that's lights out by 11.
  • Aim for the range most nights. One short night doesn't undo anything. It's the regular pattern the research measures.
  • Track it for a couple of weeks. Most people sleep less than they think, and seeing the real number is often what changes it.
