Hearty — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 16 November 2026

The short version

Hearty keeps everything you log on your phone. It sends anonymous counts of which screens and milestones are reached, never what you log or who you are, and you can turn that off in Settings. Anything else is sent only if you turn on backup, open Hearty Pro or the coin bank to see or buy something, or join the tips & updates email list, and each of those is your choice.

What Hearty stores on your phone

  • About you — your name, optionally a last name, your age, height, weight, gender, and the kind of work day you have. Optionally a profile photo, which stays in the app's own folder on the phone.
  • What you log — steps, water, meals, mindfulness minutes, sleep times, workouts and lifting sets, weigh-ins, food entries, and pulse checks you count yourself. Sleep may come from your own entry or, if you connect it, from Apple Health.
  • Your progress — XP, level, heart health, goals, reminder times, and whether you prefer dark mode.

Two dates that stay after you delete Hearty

Hearty keeps two dates in your iPhone's secure storage (the Keychain): when your free trial began, and the latest date Hearty was opened. Unlike everything else, these stay on your iPhone if you delete the app, so that reinstalling doesn't start a second free trial. They are never sent anywhere, never included in a backup, and never copied to iCloud. Nothing else about you is kept this way.

If you turn on backup

Backup is optional and off until you sign in. It exists so that a new phone, or a reinstall, can get your history back.

When you sign in, we email a code to the address you give us to check the account is yours. That address is stored so you can sign back in later.

Before the code is sent, a quick check that you are a person and not a script opens in a sign-in sheet. It is run by Cloudflare (Turnstile) on a page hosted by GitHub, and both see your IP address and basic browser details, as any web page does; neither is given your email address. The email itself is delivered by Brevo, an email service, which receives your address in order to deliver it.

Tapping "Save my progress" sends a copy of everything listed above to our database, which is hosted by Supabase. It is stored under your account, and the database is configured so that only you, signed in, can read it.

Your heart readings are never included. Heart rate, resting heart rate and heart rate variability are read from Apple Health each time you look at them and are never saved by Hearty, so there is nothing of them to upload.

Sleep is different. If Apple Health has a night your watch or phone recorded, Hearty saves it the same way it saves a night you logged by hand — so once it is in your history it is part of a backup like everything else.

Nothing is sent automatically, on a schedule, or in the background. A backup happens only when you ask for one.

Deleting your account, from the same screen, erases your backup and your email address from the server. That cannot be undone.

If you open Hearty Pro, subscribe or buy coins

Hearty Pro and coin packs are sold through the App Store. Apple takes the payment, and Hearty never sees your card details or your Apple ID.

To know whether you subscribe, and to buy coin packs, Hearty uses RevenueCat, a service that checks App Store purchases. Hearty contacts it only at these times: while the Hearty Pro screen or the Heart Shop's coin bank is open; while you subscribe, when the app starts, so that renewals and cancellations are kept in step; and, if you buy coins while signed in to backup or have bought them, when you open Profile › Your account or restore a backup that has bought coins in it.

What RevenueCat receives: a random identifier it creates for this install of Hearty, the App Store's record of your Hearty purchases, and the basic details any app's request carries — your device model, iOS version, Hearty's version, your App Store country and your IP address.

Bought coins follow your backup account. If you have bought coins and are signed in to backup, Hearty also gives RevenueCat your backup account's identifier, so the coins you paid for can come back on a new phone. That identifier is a random code our database uses for the account: it is not your email address and says nothing about you. When you restore a backup that has bought coins in it, Hearty asks RevenueCat which packs your account really bought, and only those come back. Deleting your account erases the account that code belongs to, so it no longer leads to anyone; the record of the purchase itself stays with Apple and RevenueCat, as it does for any App Store purchase.

It never receives your name, your email address, your health data, or anything you log in Hearty.

If you join the tips & updates list

After a 7-day streak, Hearty offers once to email you habit tips and news about the app. It is optional, Hearty works the same either way, and nothing is sent unless you type your email address, tick the box and tap Join.

What is sent: your email address, the date you joined, and the sentence you agreed to. It is stored in our database, hosted by Supabase, on a list kept apart from backup accounts — joining one does not sign you up for the other — and the app has no way to read the list back.

To send the emails, the list is given to Brevo, our email service. It is used only for Hearty's own tips and updates: never sold, never shared with anyone else, and never combined with anything you log in Hearty.

Every email has an unsubscribe link. To have your address removed from the list entirely, email us at hearty.app.official@gmail.com and we will delete it.

Anonymous usage counts

To see where people get stuck, Hearty reports when this install first reaches certain steps: each onboarding screen, finishing onboarding, logging for the first time, finishing every goal in a day, a 3, 7 or 30-day streak, opening the app again 1, 3, 7, 14 or 30 days after installing, opening the Hearty Pro screen, subscribing, buying coins, making a backup and joining the email list. Each step is reported once.

What is sent with each: the step's name, Hearty's version, the time, and a random code this install makes up for itself so that steps from the same phone can be counted together. That code is not linked to your name, your email, a backup account, a purchase, or anything you log, and Hearty never sends your health data, your answers or your goals with it. The counts are stored in our own database, hosted by Supabase; no outside analytics company receives them.

It is on unless you turn it off in Profile › Share anonymous usage. Turning it off stops it straight away and deletes the counts this install has sent.

If you do none of these

Then, apart from the anonymous counts above (unless you turned them off), nothing leaves your phone. Hearty contains no advertising or tracking software and no outside analytics service, and makes no other network requests with your data.

In that case Hearty has no copy of anything. Deleting the app erases everything except the two dates above, permanently, with no way to recover it.

Health data

Health and fitness data is used only to show you your own progress. It is never used for advertising or marketing, never sold, and never shared with anyone.

Permissions Hearty asks for

  • Motion & Fitness — to count your steps.
  • Apple Health — to read heart rate, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, and sleep.
  • Photos or Camera — only if you choose a profile picture.
  • Notifications — to send the reminders you set.

Every one is optional. Hearty works without them, with the matching feature switched off. You can withdraw any of them at any time in the iOS Settings app, and Hearty will stop using that data immediately.

Children

Hearty is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it.
